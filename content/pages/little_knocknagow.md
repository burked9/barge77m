# Little Knocknagow — Steam Tug (Lanarkshire c.1890)

**Little Knocknagow** (*Knocknagow II*, *the Little Knock*, or *the Little Tug*) is a historic steam tug built for inland waterways, with origins dating to the 1890s.

---

## Technical Specifications & Construction

*   **Vessel Name:** Little Knocknagow (*Knocknagow II*)
*   **Vessel Type:** Steam Tug / Tug-Barge (Flat Bottom, Counter Stern)
*   **Estimated Origin:** c. 1890s / 1900s (Lanarkshire Steel Co Ltd, Scotland)
*   **Original Dimensions:** 67 ft 6 in (L) x 12 ft 6 in (B) (including 6" wooden rubbing strakes)
*   **Shortened Dimensions:** 61 ft (L) x 12 ft 6 in (B) x 4 ft (Draught) — *Shortened by 6'6" to fit Grand Canal locks.*
*   **Machinery History:** Steam Engine → 15hp Bolinder → Nuffield Tractor engine → BMC 5.1L 6-cylinder → JCB 84hp (fitted 2009)
*   **Current Mooring:** Dromineer, Lough Derg

---

## Historical Narrative

### Origins & Construction (c. 1890s)
There is no surviving yard record of where or when *Little Knocknagow* was built, but her straight sides, flat bottom, and deep stern (drawing 4 feet) indicate she was built for inland tug-barge service. Two steel plates on the stern bear the raised inscription **"Lanarkshire Steel Co Ltd Scotland"**.

Her original dimensions—67 ft 6 in by 12 ft 6 in—suggest she may have passed through the Forth & Clyde Canal before crossing the Irish Sea to reach the River Suir, similar to the passage taken by the *Rose Macrone* in 1911. Her pronounced counter stern made her ideal for heavy towing work.

### Suir Carrying & Towing Operations
Operating out of Waterford in the early 1900s under the **Donovan family**, she carried limestone from Kilmacow quarries up the Barrow to New Ross and towed goods lighters between the railway goods yard and ships along the quays. 

After the opening of the Waterford to Rosslare Railway in 1906, she joined the **Dowleys fleet** of Carrick-on-Suir. Known as *Knocknagow II* (alongside the larger *Big Knocknagow / Knocknagow I*), she towed unpowered sand and gravel lighters and general cargo barges up the Suir at 2 mph.

### Engine History & Working Crews
Originally steam-powered (with Pat Reilly as an early engineer), her steam engine was replaced by a **15hp pre-heated Bolinder diesel**. **James Jacques** served as engineer for over forty years across both the steam and diesel eras until a tragic drowning in winter floods at Fiddown Bridge in 1958.

Skippers of *Little Knocknagow* included:
*   **J. Healy** (First recorded skipper)
*   **Mikey Dowley**
*   **Johnny Norris** (Skipper for ~20 years and Chairman of Carrick UDC in 1947)
*   **Tim Sweeney**
*   **Tom Sweeney**
*   **Paddy Sweeney**

### Sand Dredging & Coastal Voyages (1960s – 1970s)
In the 1960s, she was sold to O'Keeffes of Mooncoin, and later acquired by **Roadstone Wexford** for sand dredging at Brownswood Jetty on the River Slaney. Managed by inter-county hurler **Nick O'Donnell**, a mechanical shovel was fitted to her bow to extract up to 100 tons of aggregate daily for Wexford concrete. 

After sinking once at Eddermine Bridge with her Bolinder running, a **Nuffield tractor engine** (without wheels) was fitted in her engine room to drive the propeller shaft via power take-off.

### Preservation by Richard Miller (1975 – 2004)
In 1975, Wexford farmer **Richard Miller** saved *Knocknagow* from scrap. To allow her to navigate the Grand Canal locks, he shorten her by **6 ft 6 in** (to 61 ft) on the US flying-boat slipway in Wexford. He re-engined her with a **BMC 5.1L 6-cylinder diesel**, rebuilt her stern for a 20" propeller, fitted two sailing masts (with square and gaff sails), and overplated her hull in steel. 

She completed coastal trips to West Cork and Scotsman's Bay (Dun Laoghaire), and served as mother-ship for Wexford Harbour regattas.

### Transfer to Lough Derg (2004 – Present)
In April 2004, *Little Knocknagow* was purchased by **Anne and Brian Goggin**. To reduce her deep draft for the Barrow navigation, Richard Miller strapped six 40-gallon air barrels under the stern, lifting her 9 inches to safely navigate the river to Vicarstown.

She was transferred to **Lough Derg** and based at **Dromineer**, where a new **JCB 84hp engine** was installed in 2009. She remains a cherished surviving icon of Ireland's floating heritage.
