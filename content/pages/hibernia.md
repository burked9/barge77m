# Canal Boat 77.M (Hibernia) — Captain's Logbook & Restoration

Welcome to the dedicated record for **77.M** (Hibernia), one of the last remaining workboats—and later dredgers—of the Irish Inland Waterways.

> *"Welcome to the Captain's Logbook and Restoration details of canal boat 77.M! This record documents all journeys taken since purchasing the barge over twelve months ago, tracks ongoing voyages, uploads photographs of working season restoration, and preserves the boat's long and detailed history."*

## Vessel Specifications & Construction

*   **Official Name & Number:** 77.M (Hibernia)
*   **Year Built:** 1937
*   **Builder:** Ringsend Dockyard Company (McMillen Boat)
*   **Dimensions:** 61 ft 9 in (L) x 13 ft 3 in (B) — *Built slightly longer and wider than standard M-boats alongside sister ships 78M and 79M.*
*   **Original Engine:** Bolinder (Later replaced with 59hp air-cooled Lister diesel engine)
*   **Historical Roles:** Commercial Carrying Boat, CIE Workboat, Priestman Dredger, Historic Vessel
*   **Current Location:** Church Bay, Lough Derg, Co. Clare

## The History of 77.M (1937 – Present)

### Construction & Early Years (1937 – 1950s)
77.M was built in 1937 by the **Ringsend Dockyard Company** in Dublin and was known as a *"McMillen boat"* after her boat builder. Together with her sister ships **78M** and **79M**, she was built slightly longer and wider than the standard M-boat specification, measuring **61 ft 9 ins by 13 ft 3 ins**.

In the early 1950s, **Eddie Cummins** from Edenderry worked alongside **Paddy Aungier** on 77.M. Tragic events marked this era when poor Eddie drowned off 77.M at Daingean around Christmas in 1954/5 while loading beet.

Another tragedy linked to 77.M occurred below Portumna when **George Fitzpatrick** from Dublin drowned while towing a rowboat for a local man from Garrykennedy. George pulled the rowboat up to the barge to climb back aboard 77.M, but pulling on the towrope at the bow pulled down the nose of the tender, casting him into the lake. His body was recovered by another vessel the following day.

### CIE Working Crews & Service
Over her commercial carrying years, 77.M was crewed by legendary canal men. At one period, **Robert Mahon** served as skipper alongside **Paddy Mahon** and **Paddy Delaney**. 

Her final commercial crew under Grand Canal / CIE carrying operations were all from Banagher:
*   **Andy Maloney** — Skipper (In Charge)
*   **Tom "Thick" Donnelan** — Driver / Engineman
*   **Will "Abie" Maloney** — Deckhand

### Conversion to Dredger (1960s – 1990s)
Unlike many M-boats that were sold off when commercial canal traffic ceased, 77.M was retained by CIE and converted into a **dredger**. Her bow was cut down to the waterline and fitted with a **Priestman dredger**. Her original Bolinder engine was replaced with a **59hp air-cooled Lister diesel engine**.

Her first dredging crew comprised **Tommy Doyle** and **Christy Daly**. Over the following decades, she was worked by **John Doherty**, **John Whelan** (of Graiguenamanagh), and **Joe Logan**. 

In **1986–1987**, **Kit Moran** and **Ger Nolan** crewed 77.M while she worked clearing the Circular Line in Dublin. By **1995**, she moved to work on the River Barrow before being formally retired from service in **1997**.

### Preservation, Auction & Ongoing Restoration (2014 – Present)
Following her retirement, 77.M was taken into the care of **Waterways Ireland**. She was initially stored in the canal outside the WI Maintenance Depot at **Fenniscourt Lock**, and later lifted into the yard for safekeeping.

In **2014**, she was put up for auction. Current skipper **Daniel Burke** purchased 77.M, refloated her, and transferred her to **Church Bay on Lough Derg in County Clare**, where she is currently undergoing active restoration to preserve her as one of the last surviving workboats of the Irish Inland Waterways.
