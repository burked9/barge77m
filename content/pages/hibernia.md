# Barge Hibernia — History & Community Legacy

## Vessel Overview & Specifications

*   **Official Name:** Hibernia (*HI - BERNIA*)
*   **Year Built:** 1912
*   **Builder:** Portadown Foundry
*   **Dimensions:** 63 ft (L) x 15 ft (B)
*   **Original Machinery:** Bolinder Engine -> 11L AEC Mandator -> Perkins 6354 with Perkins 4108 Wing Engine
*   **Auxiliaries:** 15hp Bowthruster, 6kW Generator, 3kW Inverter
*   **Current Location:** Portaneena, Lough Ree / Inland Waterways

## History of Hibernia

### Early Lagan Service & Luxury Conversion (1912 – 1950s)
Built by the **Portadown Foundry in 1912**, *Hibernia* measures 63 feet long by 15 feet wide. Her initial service was as a lighter on the River Lagan for approximately four years before being acquired by a local businessman who converted her into a pleasure craft.

While living aboard old barges was not unknown, *Hibernia* was probably the earliest full luxury barge conversion in Ireland. No expense was spared during her transformation: her side decks were removed and a new steel superstructure was fitted, extending up the sides by over 30 inches and fitted with portholes. 

The top deck was covered in wood and fitted out with cut-glass doghouses. Inside, she featured tiled concrete floors, a fireplace, and domestic plumbing such as a bath. Those who remember her from the 1940s and 1950s recall that she was fitted out like a palace.

### Submergence on the Bann & The "Mud Bucket" Refloating (1964 – 2003)
In 1964, *Hibernia* was moved to the Upper Bann just below the M1 motorway bridge, where she was later vandalized and eventually sank. She remained submerged for nearly four decades.

Years later, a prospective barge owner spotted her sunken hull from the M1 bridge. After tracking down her owner, a deal was struck. It took a dedicated crew several months to clear tens of tonnes of mud from her hull, earning her the affectionate nickname **"The Mud Bucket"**.

During heavy rainfall one night, the suction of the mud under her hull finally let go and *Hibernia* refloated herself, slipping her moorings and floating a mile downstream before being secured. She was towed by Mick Savage's vessel *Naomi* to Battery Harbour for full cleaning, before being towed across Lough Neagh to Toome for hull restoration.

### Engine History & Ulster Canal Heritage
*   **Original Engine:** Bolinder diesel
*   **Second Engine:** 11-litre AEC Mandator engine
*   **Current Engine:** Perkins 6354 main engine with a Perkins 4108 wing engine
*   **Electrical & Thruster:** 15hp Bowthruster, 6kW generator, and 3kW inverter

*Hibernia* was lovingly restored over six years in Toome, waiting patiently to lead the fleet from Lough Neagh to the Erne when the Ulster Canal reopens.

### Transfer to Lough Ree (2018 – Present)
In the Autumn of 2018, *Hibernia* was transported by road from Lough Neagh to **Portaneena on Lough Ree** for redesign and construction works under new ownership, continuing her heritage journey as one of Ireland's most historic floating craft.
